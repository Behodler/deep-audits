## Finding description and impact

### Summary

`batchMint` grants the pinned minter an unbounded allowance for the duration of the mint loop ([`BatchNFTMinter.sol#L360`](https://github.com/Behodler/phoenix-nft-staking/blob/main/src/BatchNFTMinter.sol#L360)):

```solidity
// --- 5. Pull the caller's payment budget. ---
paymentToken.safeTransferFrom(msg.sender, address(this), paymentAmount);

// --- 6. Approve the pinned minter for the loop. ---
paymentToken.forceApprove(address(nftMinter), type(uint256).max);

// --- 7. Mint loop. ---
for (uint256 i; i < count; ++i) {
    nftMinter.mint(_dispatcherIndex, recipient);
}
```

The approval covers the whole loop rather than the caller's actual budget, and `NFTMinterV2` pulls from the **batcher's** balance rather than from a per-caller ledger. An under-funded batch therefore does **not** revert as the contract documents; the shortfall is silently covered by whatever payment token the contract already holds.

### Vulnerability details

The contract's own NatSpec states the intended behaviour at [`#L69-L71`](https://github.com/Behodler/phoenix-nft-staking/blob/main/src/BatchNFTMinter.sol#L69-L71):

> *If `paymentAmount` falls short of the dispatcher's cumulative charge an inner `mint` reverts and the entire batch atomically rolls back.*

That guarantee holds only when the contract's standing payment-token balance is zero. It is not, in the steady state, zero: the same NatSpec describes the contract as receiving third-party donations, and §3 explicitly retains sub-`DUST_THRESHOLD` residue between batches ("*Sub-threshold residue is intentionally left to absorb JS-side rounding noise and is picked up by the next batch's sweep*", [`#L64-L68`](https://github.com/Behodler/phoenix-nft-staking/blob/main/src/BatchNFTMinter.sol#L64-L68)).

The attack requires no privilege, no timing window and no reentrancy:

1. The contract holds a non-zero payment-token balance — donations, accumulated dust, or the nudge pot itself.
2. A caller submits `batchMint` with `count = N` but `paymentAmount` sufficient for only `M < N` mints.
3. The `type(uint256).max` approval covers the whole loop, not the caller's budget.
4. `NFTMinterV2._executeMint` pulls from the batcher's balance, so mints `M+1 … N` are funded by the contract's own holdings.
5. The batch does **not** revert. The caller receives `N` NFTs for `M` mints' worth of payment, and `totalPaid` under-reports the true dispatcher cost.

Two structural notes matter for remediation:

- **This is a second, independent extraction route to the same pot as M-01** (`fcaca002…`, the step-10 sweep). The code-scanner's finding record states it explicitly: *the sweep-bound fix does not close the max-allowance route.* Fixing the sweep leaves this path open at full magnitude while the pot reads as protected.
- **The only thing containing the max allowance lives in another repository.** The bound is `NFTMinterV2._executeMint` pulling from `msg.sender` — a cross-repo invariant that `BatchNFTMinter` neither asserts nor tests. A change on that side turns an unbounded approval into an unbounded exposure with no signal here.

### Impact

Permissionless value leak from the contract's own balance: a caller obtains `count` NFTs for less than `count` mints' worth of payment, with the difference paid from third-party donations, inter-batch dust, and the nudge pot. It is also a spec deviation — the documented atomic-rollback guarantee at `#L69-L71` and §3 steps 5/6 is false whenever the contract holds a balance.

### Severity rationale and honest bounds

**Medium**: a permissionless, executed, PoC-proven value leak with stated assumptions and an external requirement, against a pot this run rates Medium elsewhere. Not High — no principal is stolen, no third-party deposit is at risk, and the extractable amount is bounded by the contract's standing balance.

Bounds, stated plainly:

- **Magnitude is small today.** `RatchetBatchNFTMinter` holds zero of everything; the successor instance `0x86866e01…` holds 219.99 USDC (D-22 mainnet reads, block 25572875). A **Low** reading is defensible purely on magnitude, and the finding is flagged as disputed on that basis. The Medium rests on the standalone C4 ground above — a permissionless, PoC-proven value leak with stated assumptions and a cross-repo external requirement — not on the present balance. Independence from M-01's fix is recorded below as a do-not-collapse constraint, not as a severity ground.
- The finding was escalated from the scanner's proposed Low under D-10.
- A suppression under ledger L-01's severity note (`9135cf7947…` — the ledger carries five `L-01`s; disambiguate by fingerprint) was **declined**: that note's premise ("only value-moving reentrancy paths require honest-caller misconfiguration") is scoped to reentrancy paths. No reentry is involved here at all.
- Do not collapse with **DEDUP-20-002**.

## Recommended mitigation steps

Approve exactly the caller's budget instead of an unbounded amount, and assert the post-loop spend:

```solidity
// --- 6. Approve the pinned minter for the loop, bounded by the caller's budget. ---
uint256 balBefore = paymentToken.balanceOf(address(this));
paymentToken.forceApprove(address(nftMinter), paymentAmount);

// --- 7. Mint loop. ---
for (uint256 i; i < count; ++i) {
    nftMinter.mint(_dispatcherIndex, recipient);
}

paymentToken.forceApprove(address(nftMinter), 0);

// The batch must never be funded by this contract's standing balance.
uint256 spent = balBefore - paymentToken.balanceOf(address(this));
if (spent > paymentAmount) revert BatchMint__BatchSpentContractBalance(spent, paymentAmount);
```

The post-loop assertion is the load-bearing half: it re-establishes the documented atomic-rollback guarantee even if the allowance bound is later loosened, and it does not depend on any property of `NFTMinterV2`.

**Fix this together with M-01** (`fcaca002…`). The two are independent routes to the same funds; patching either alone leaves the pot extractable while reading as closed.
