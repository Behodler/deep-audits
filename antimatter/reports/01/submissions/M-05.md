# [M-05] Shared daily budget: the annihilator extracts 2 phUSD per unit of budget and DoSes ordinary minters

**Ledger fingerprint:** `6a6a6bcba7b0dff1`
**Severity:** Medium
**Location:** `lib/antimatter/src/Antimatter.sol:227` (the shared budget charge); context `:210-233`
**Root cause class:** `SharedDailyBudgetAsymmetricExtraction`

## Finding description and impact

### Summary

`annihilateFrom` charges `PhusdStableMinter`'s per-stablecoin daily budget at `Antimatter.sol:227` — the **same** counter the protocol's ordinary permissionless stablecoin → phUSD on-ramp charges. But the two paths extract from that shared budget at different rates: an ordinary minter receives 1 phUSD per unit of budget consumed, an annihilator receives 2 (per M-03, the antimatter leg at `:236` is issued without charging the counter).

The result is a structural incentive defect: exhausting the shared budget is not a cost the annihilator pays to grief, it is a **side effect of the most profitable use of that budget**. A rational annihilator does it by default.

### Vulnerability details

```solidity
minter.mint(stable, stableAmount);   // :227  charges the SAME per-stable daily counter as the ordinary on-ramp
```

1. The annihilator holds antimatter and the registered stablecoin.
2. They call `annihilateFrom` sized to consume the remaining `config.mintedToday` headroom at `:227`.
3. Ordinary depositors are refused with `"Daily mint limit exceeded"` for the rest of the 24h window, while the annihilator has extracted 2x per unit of budget consumed.
4. Because the window restarts on the **first mint after expiry** rather than on a fixed schedule, the annihilator — by choosing when to place that first mint — also controls when each subsequent window opens.

### Impact

**Availability, not assets.** The protocol's ordinary permissionless on-ramp becomes deniable for a given stablecoin for the remainder of a 24h window, by an actor the protocol is simultaneously **paying** to do it. No user loses funds, and the condition self-resolves each window; but the denial has zero marginal cost to the attacker because it is inseparable from a trade they want to make anyway.

The window-reset semantics compound this by handing the annihilator control over the timing of each window's opening, so the denial can be positioned rather than merely repeated.

### Severity justification

**Medium** on the C4 availability limb: "the function of the protocol or its availability could be impacted."

Not High: there is no theft, no user loses funds, and the condition self-resolves at the window boundary. The external requirements are real and are stated rather than priced in optimistically — antimatter emission must be live (the same caveat as M-02: antimatter is not yet wired into `phoenix-phase-2-staging`, so emission policy is undecided on-chain), and `maxMintPerDay` must be non-zero. That combination is exactly what C4 Medium permits and what keeps this off High.

Not Low: denying the protocol's general on-ramp at zero marginal cost to a party the protocol is paying is a structural incentive defect, not an incidental one.

**Confidence: medium**, recorded as such — the behaviour is demonstrated, but its practical severity depends on emission volumes that are not yet decided.

**Assumptions:** `maxMintPerDay` is set non-zero for the stablecoin; the annihilator holds enough antimatter to consume the window's budget.

Deliberately **not collapsed** with M-03. Both concern the same counter, but the root causes differ (under-counting versus profitable budget exhaustion), the impact classes differ (control mis-sizing versus availability), and the mitigations differ. Collapsing them would bury the denial-of-service.

### Corroborating evidence

The 2x extraction asymmetry underlying this finding is independently established by M-03's invariant failures on two engines (`invariant_09`, `invariant_10`, Foundry + Medusa) and by Medusa's single-call counterexample pinning the ratio at exactly 2.000x. The anti-vacuity check recorded there — 121 swallowed inner reverts dominated by `"Daily mint limit exceeded"` — also confirms the cap is genuinely live and enforcing, which is the precondition for the denial described here.

## Recommended mitigation

1. **Separate or proportion the budgets.** Either give the annihilation path its own per-period budget, so it cannot consume the general on-ramp's headroom at all; or charge the antimatter leg to the shared counter as well (which also fixes M-03), so both paths consume budget in proportion to the phUSD they issue. The second option is preferable — it removes the asymmetry that creates the incentive, rather than only relocating the contention.
2. **Use a fixed epoch boundary** (e.g. `block.timestamp / 1 days`) instead of restarting the window on the first mint after expiry. This removes the annihilator's control over when each window opens, and makes the on-ramp's availability schedule predictable for ordinary users.

Optionally, consider a per-caller or per-window share limit on the on-ramp so no single actor can consume an entire window's headroom in one transaction; this is a mitigation for the concentration, not for the underlying asymmetry, and should not substitute for (1).
