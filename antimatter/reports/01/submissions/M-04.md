# [M-04] `exchangeRate` re-prices only the stable leg: a hard 1x issuance floor and a near-zero-rate brick

**Ledger fingerprint:** `abe4305ac8f0c44f`
**Severity:** Medium
**Location:** `lib/antimatter/src/Antimatter.sol:236` (rate-independent antimatter leg), `:254-256` (rate-independent stablecoin pull), `:250` (config read); context `:236-256`
**Root cause class:** `ExchangeRateRepricesOnlyStableLeg`

## Finding description and impact

### Summary

`exchangeRate` is the obvious lever an operator would reach for to throttle phUSD issuance. It does not work as one. The quantity of stablecoin **pulled** is rate-independent (a decimals-only rescale, `:254-256`), and the antimatter leg mints exactly `amount` regardless of the rate (`:236`). Only the stable leg's phUSD is rate-scaled. Total issuance per unit of stablecoin received is therefore `(1 + exchangeRate/1e18)`, which is bounded **below by 1x** and cannot be reduced past it by any setting.

### Vulnerability details

```solidity
uint256 scale = 10 ** (18 - decimals);      // :254  rate plays no part
uint256 stableAmount = amount / scale;      // :255
if (stableAmount * scale != amount) revert AmountNotRepresentable(amount, decimals);  // :256
...
_phUSD.mint(recipient, amount);             // :236  always exactly `amount`, whatever the rate
```

Three distinct consequences follow, of which two are the basis for this severity:

**(a) A hard 1x floor — the throttle cannot reach parity.** At `exchangeRate = 0.10e18` the protocol still emits 1.10 phUSD per $1 taken in. The uncollateralised component (M-02) is entirely unaffected by the rate at any setting. There is **no** rate that makes annihilation backing-neutral. The only true stop is `setStablecoinEnabled(false)` — a full stop with no intermediate setting between "always dilutive" and "off".

**(b) Pushing the rate toward zero bricks rather than throttles.** Below the point where the stable leg's phUSD truncates to zero, `annihilateFrom` reverts with `PhUSDNotReceived` (`:233`). The lever is a cliff, not a dial: a no-op down to the cliff, then a hard revert past it. The revert region is not a single witness but a whole region — at `exchangeRate = 1 wei`, every 18dp amount whose 6dp form is below `1e6` reverts identically.

**(c) A rate change silently re-prices a different control.** Because the daily cap's effective multiplier is `(1 + exchangeRate/1e18)` (M-03), moving the rate to `0.5e18` shifts that multiplier from 2x to 3x the entered number, with no change to `maxMintPerDay` and no documentation of the coupling on either side. The M-03 mis-sizing drifts underneath the operator.

### This is a Law-3 owner footgun, not an admin-abuse finding

No malicious-owner vector is claimed. The **obvious** half of this — an owner *raising* the rate and getting more issuance (`rate = 1.5e18` emits 2.5 phUSD per $1) — is exactly what Law 3 suppresses as an obvious consequence, and it carries no severity of its own here.

The Medium rests entirely on the two **surprising** halves: that *lowering* the rate can never reach parity, so a safety lever silently cannot do the job it appears to do; and that a rate change silently re-prices a separate control. A competent, non-malicious operator would be surprised by both. Surprise ⇒ footgun ⇒ in scope at honest severity.

### Impact

No direct asset loss. Two operational impacts:

1. The natural remediation lever for over-issuance does not work. An operator responding to observed dilution engages the throttle, believes it is working, and dilution continues at ≥ 1x indefinitely — and if they push it far enough, the path bricks with a revert rather than throttling.
2. Changing the rate silently re-scales what `maxMintPerDay` permits, compounding M-03 at exactly the moment the operator is intervening.

The hazard bites precisely when an operator is responding to an issuance problem — i.e. at the worst possible moment.

### Severity justification

**Medium** as an operational hazard. "The protocol's issuance control does not function as a control" is a C4 Medium function impact. Not High: no theft, no direct asset risk, an owner action is required, and the harm is realised only through the dilution channel already priced in M-02. Not Low, because the impaired thing is the remediation lever for the protocol's largest structural exposure.

**Assumption:** the operator treats `exchangeRate` as an issuance throttle, which is what its name and position suggest.
**External requirement:** an owner rate change — non-malicious, and non-obvious in its consequences.

## Safe-configuration guidance

- Treat `exchangeRate` as an **amplitude control on the backed leg only**, never as an issuance throttle.
- Keep it at or below `1e18` on every annihilatable stablecoin.
- Recompute `maxMintPerDay` as `intendedIssuance / (1 + exchangeRate/1e18)` on **every** rate change (see M-03).
- Do not push the rate toward zero expecting a soft throttle: below the truncation threshold the path reverts with `PhUSDNotReceived` rather than issuing less.
- To actually stop dilution, use `setStablecoinEnabled(false)`. That is the only lever that works.

### Corroborating evidence

Halmos **PROVED** the no-residue property holds for **every** `uint256 exchangeRate` (`reports/antimatter/01/tier3/halmos-p2-noresidue-unbounded.txt`) — an arbitrary rate does not strand assets on the contract. This is cited as **scoping evidence, not reassurance**: it isolates the hazard as economic and operational rather than a settlement defect.

Profitability boundary carried from M-02: the annihilation loop stays profitable whenever `p > 1/(1 + rate/1e18)` — `p > $1.00` at rate 0, `p > $0.50` at rate `1e18`.

## Recommended mitigation

1. **Make the rate govern the whole output.** Scale the antimatter leg by the same rate at `:236`, so a single knob controls total issuance and lowering it can actually reach — and pass — parity. This is the fix that makes the lever behave the way its name promises.
2. **Or, fail closed on an out-of-band result.** Antimatter already reads `exchangeRate`'s sibling fields at `:250`; add a sanity bound asserting `mintedForStable` is within tolerance of `amount`, so a fat-fingered or oversized rate reverts instead of silently issuing an unintended multiple.
3. **Replace the unbounded `mintedForStable == 0` check at `:233`** with a two-sided assertion against `minter.calculateMintAmount(stable, stableAmount)`. This converts the near-zero-rate brick from an opaque `PhUSDNotReceived` into an explicit, diagnosable mismatch. (Shared mitigation with L-01 and F-01.)
4. **Document the coupling on both sides:** that `maxMintPerDay`'s effective multiplier is `(1 + exchangeRate/1e18)`, and that a rate change re-prices it.
