# [M-02] The antimatter leg mints phUSD against zero collateral; the liability is unbounded and unaccounted

**Ledger fingerprint:** `64807f2b1456d622`
**Severity:** Medium
**Location:** `lib/antimatter/src/Antimatter.sol:236` (the unbacked mint), `:179` (uncapped antimatter emission); context `:179-239`
**Root cause class:** `UnbackedMintLegUnaccounted`

## Finding description and impact

### Summary

Every annihilation mints `amount` phUSD at `Antimatter.sol:236` against **no incoming value**. The stable leg is genuinely collateralised — the stablecoin is pulled and deposited into a yield strategy — but the antimatter leg is a bare mint on Antimatter's own FlaxToken authorisation. The resulting liability is unbounded, unscheduled, and recorded nowhere.

This is filed as **measured dilution of a designed behaviour**, not as theft. Nobody's assets are taken; the annihilator holds antimatter and parts with real stablecoin. What the report quantifies is that the design issues phUSD against zero backing at a fixed 1:1 ratio to antimatter burned, with no on-chain bound and no on-chain measurement of the accumulated exposure.

### Vulnerability details

```solidity
_phUSD.mint(recipient, amount);                                    // :236  no incoming value
IERC20(address(_phUSD)).safeTransfer(recipient, mintedForStable);  // :237  backed by the deposited stablecoin
```

and upstream:

```solidity
function mint(address to, uint256 amount) external onlyApprovedMinters {   // :179
    _mint(to, amount);
}
```

Antimatter emission at `:179` has no supply cap, no per-period budget, and no tie to realised yield or collateral. Since each antimatter token is permanently exercisable into ~$1 of unbacked phUSD via `:236`, **`Antimatter.totalSupply()` is exactly the protocol's outstanding unbacked-phUSD liability** — a quantity no contract in the system reads, reports, or bounds.

The economics, stated without dramatisation:

1. An actor receives N antimatter as a staking reward (`:179`, gated to approved minters).
2. The actor acquires N (rescaled) stablecoin at ~$1 — real capital they must part with.
3. `annihilateFrom(stable, self, self, N)`: burn N antimatter, surrender $N of stablecoin, receive ~2N phUSD.
4. Sell 2N phUSD into protocol-owned liquidity at price p. Gross of the antimatter itself, the flow is 2Np − N.
5. Recycle.

**This flow is not a standing free-money claim, and is not presented as one.** *(Corrected in the 2026-08-18 review pass: the earlier "profitable for any phUSD price > $0.50" reading double-counted, by treating the antimatter leg as free.)* The antimatter consumed at step 1 is itself worth roughly its exercise value, `max(0, 2p − 1)` per unit; once it is priced by a market rather than treated as a costless grant, arbitrage drives the round-trip return toward that value and the excess profit toward zero. What survives the correction — and what this finding actually reports — is not an arbitrage yield figure but the **issuance structure**: the ~2x mint at `:236` is issued against no incoming value, and it ships with **no cap, no per-period budget, and no on-chain view of the resulting liability**. The exposure is real whether or not any particular actor books a profit on it.

### Precisely what kind of loss this is — and is not

This must not be read as a "value leak" in the yield-funded sense. The standing Phoenix rule that *externally-derived yield over-payment is opportunity cost, not economic loss* was checked and **does not apply here**. That rule concerns reward budgets funded by yield on protocol-owned capital, where over-payment is misallocation or marketing spend. This is a different thing: phUSD is minted at `:236` **against zero incoming value**, which is real dilution of the backing per phUSD unit. The collateral pool does not grow to match the new supply. The harm falls on non-consenting phUSD holders through the collateral-ratio channel, not on a reward budget.

It is equally not theft: the mint is the token's documented purpose, and the actor gives up real capital to trigger it.

### Impact

An unbounded, permanent, unaccounted liability against the phUSD collateral base. phUSD has **no redemption path anywhere** (verified across the tree), which cuts both ways: it means the exposure never trips an insolvency check that would force attention, and instead surfaces only as peg pressure — with no on-chain control over the exposure and not even an on-chain *observation* of it.

The PoC measures the curve: sustained annihilation drives the backing ratio asymptotically toward 0.50, and 200,000,000e18 phUSD of the terminal supply is unbacked.

**The reportable core, stated without inflation:** unbacked issuance ships with **no cap**, **no budget**, and **no liability view**. `Antimatter.totalSupply()` is the outstanding unbacked-phUSD liability and no contract reads, reports, or bounds it. No claim is made here about a standing arbitrage profit rate — see the correction under the economics above.

### Severity justification

**Medium**, weighed explicitly against both an upgrade and a downgrade.

Not **High**: there is no atomic attack and no specific victim. Annihilation is one-way, the actor must actually hold antimatter and actually part with real stablecoin, and the harm is diffuse dilution realised only through a market sale. Decisively for the current tree, emission is owner-gated — only approved minters mint antimatter — so no permissionless drain exists today, and antimatter is not yet wired into `phoenix-phase-2-staging`. Labelling a policy exposure "High" would be overstatement.

Not **Low/QA**: the liability is real, permanent, unbounded and completely unaccounted, and lands on parties who never consented. An unmeasured unbounded liability is not a state-handling nit. C4 Medium — "value leak with stated assumptions and external requirements" — fits.

**Intended design is not a mitigant.** Law 1 overrides Law 2: the spec status is FAITHFUL-BUT-UNSAFE. The declared intent at `:188-189` and in the project's `CLAUDE.md` is implemented precisely; the intent itself is the hazard. In-source NatSpec carries no suppression authority, and the header NatSpec describing this mint is itself economically inaccurate (tracked separately as F-03).

**Assumptions:** antimatter is emitted in quantity; there is liquidity to sell into. No assumption about a minimum phUSD price is required for the finding — the price only affects how any individual actor books the round trip, not the fact that the issuance is uncapped, unbudgeted and unmeasured.
**External requirement:** an approved minter must actually emit antimatter via `setApprovedMinter` (`:151`).

### Re-weigh triggers

- **Escalate to HIGH** if antimatter emission is ever wired to a **permissionless or automated/high-volume minter** via `setApprovedMinter` (`:151`). The owner gate is the only thing holding this at Medium; once step 1 of the loop stops being owner-gated, this becomes an active self-serve drain on the phUSD collateral base rather than a policy exposure.
- **Secondary:** a bug in **any** approved minter contract converts directly into unbacked phUSD, because `:179` has no supply ceiling.
- **Tertiary:** if a phUSD redemption path is ever built, the exposure converts from peg pressure into on-chain insolvency and must be re-weighed immediately.

### Corroborating evidence — quantification, not reassurance

`invariant_04_unbackedPhusdEqualsAntimatterBurned` **passes** as an exact equality over 512 runs / 76,800 calls (`reports/antimatter-01/tier3/invariant-results.md:33`): `phUSD.totalSupply()` minus stablecoin in custody (18dp-normalised) equals cumulative antimatter burned. This pass is cited **only as a measurement** — it establishes that the uncollateralised leg is exactly 1:1 with antimatter burned with no rounding drift. It is not an all-clear, and no part of this severity rests on it. `invariant_05` (same run depth) separately confirms every stablecoin unit pulled reached strategy custody, i.e. the *stable* leg is not the problem.

Scope limit stated honestly: invariants 04/05 pin `exchangeRate` at `1e18`; rate divergence is M-04's territory.

## Recommended mitigation

1. **Measure the liability.** Treat `Antimatter.totalSupply()` as the protocol's outstanding unbacked-phUSD liability and expose it as such — at minimum a view reporting it alongside `phUSD.totalSupply()`, so the exposure is observable on-chain instead of inferable only off-chain.
2. **Bound emission at the source.** Add a supply cap or per-period mint budget to `mint` at `:179`, so the liability cannot be enlarged without limit by a single call from any approved minter. This is the control whose absence is the difference between a bounded design choice and an unbounded one.
3. **Encode the funding policy on-chain.** If the intended policy is that antimatter emission is budgeted against realised protocol yield, enforce that on-chain — the code currently has no representation of the policy at all, so nothing prevents emission from outrunning it.
4. **Correct the NatSpec** at `:18`, which describes this mint in terms that are economically inaccurate (tracked separately as F-03).

Note that mitigations 1-3 do not change the designed behaviour; they bound and instrument it. If the protocol intends the 2x issuance, that intent is compatible with a cap and a public liability figure.
