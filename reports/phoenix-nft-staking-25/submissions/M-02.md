> ## Why it is withdrawn
> 
> The nudge pot is funded by **externally-derived yield on protocol-owned capital** (the Phoenix
> protocol owns its capital and earns yield on it from external vaults such as Tokemak; that yield
> flows to `phlimbo` and to the `batchMinter` nudge pot). The pot is **never** funded from principal
> and **never** from user deposits.
>
> Consequently, topping the pot beyond the cost of a qualifying batch is **opportunity cost** — that
> yield could instead have gone straight into liquidity growth — and **not economic loss**. It is an
> accepted short-run cost and a deliberate **marketing spend**: a visible profit opportunity pulls
> users in, and the deficit closes as the lowest-bidding marginal user snipes the mint. `NudgeStreamer`
> meters release precisely so the market finds a clearing price. **A large pot is the mechanism
> working, not a failure.** Empirically, in live operation the pot has **never once reached even 50 %
> of the qualifying cost** before someone minted.
>
> This report assumed the shape an auditor reflexively assumes — *owner puts up principal, a fraction
> returns*. That shape is wrong for this protocol. The finding is withdrawn.
>
> **What was NOT withdrawn, and remains reportable at full severity (Law 1 — this suppression is narrow):**
>
> - (a) any path where the pot leaves **without** the caller paying for `nudgeSize` real mints;
> - (b) any path where `refund > paymentAmount`;
> - (c) any path where a **non-qualifying** batch extracts pot-sized value;
> - (d) **any claimant taking other users' money** — e.g. forfeited sub-`DUST_THRESHOLD` refund residue
>   accreting into the pot (run-25 **M-03**'s channel). Value that originated with a user does not
>   become protocol subsidy by falling into the pot.
>
> Carve-outs (a)/(b)/(c) were **verified clean by run-25**, so nothing remains beneath this finding.

## Why it was merged

M-02 has **zero standalone impact**:

- Given M-01 fixed (the payout capped against the charge), recycling the searcher's own
  qualifying cost is a wash — there is nothing left to over-extract.
- Given M-02 fixed (the payment loop severed), M-01 still drains the pot.

It is a pure **amplifier** — it converts M-01 from one-shot into repeatable — and it is
**fully closed by M-01's fix**, which bounds the exit rather than the entrance. M-01's own
text already conceded "a single shared fix". The merged finding is strictly stronger than
either part: the value-blind gate drains the pot once, *and* the cost recycles so it drains
perpetually.

The `d4cc563` streamer routing weakens this leg further — recovery is metered over `duration`
rather than instantaneous, one full `C` is permanently escrowed in flight, and the exploit
becomes an interruptible multi-day race. See M-01 for the corrected mechanism and the
amortisation table.
