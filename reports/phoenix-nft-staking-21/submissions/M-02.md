## Finding description and impact

### Summary

`src/BatchNFTMinterMultiToken.sol` inherits `ReentrancyGuard`
([`:82`](https://github.com/Behodler/phoenix-nft-staking/blob/c881a428c87ef4ef42ba07a71be5d49101c9006d/src/BatchNFTMinterMultiToken.sol#L82))
and marks `batchMint` `nonReentrant`
([`:300`](https://github.com/Behodler/phoenix-nft-staking/blob/c881a428c87ef4ef42ba07a71be5d49101c9006d/src/BatchNFTMinterMultiToken.sol#L300)),
with NatSpec at `:78-81` calling the guard *"required rather than optional"*. The **frozen, mainnet-deployed**
`src/BatchNFTMinter.sol` has neither
([`:62`](https://github.com/Behodler/phoenix-nft-staking/blob/c881a428c87ef4ef42ba07a71be5d49101c9006d/src/BatchNFTMinter.sol#L62),
[`:243`](https://github.com/Behodler/phoenix-nft-staking/blob/c881a428c87ef4ef42ba07a71be5d49101c9006d/src/BatchNFTMinter.sol#L243)).

The project recognised the hazard and fixed it **only on the sibling**. That asymmetry is the finding.
This is **realised fork drift**, not a design position — and the omission did not relocate, it **stayed
behind on the file that is actually deployed**.

Mid-loop, `nftMinter.mint` reaches the ERC-1155 acceptance hook `onERC1155Received` on the `recipient`.
On the deployed file, control returns to that recipient while the `type(uint256).max` allowance from
`:284` is still live and the pre-loop nudge snapshot is still unpaid.

**The nudge payout goes to `recipient` (`:301`); the sweep goes to `msg.sender` (`:307`). Both are
attacker-choosable when the attacker is the caller.** That fact makes the primary leg **self-service**:
it needs no victim at all.

### Attack path A — self-service nudge double-payment (PRIMARY: no victim, no integration)

The attacker is **both caller and recipient**. Nobody else has to do anything, and nobody else has to
have made a mistake.

1. Attacker calls `batchMint(count >= nudgeSize, recipient = own hostile contract, paymentAmount = X)`
   and funds the batch honestly out of their own pocket.
2. The acceptance hook fires on the last iteration and re-enters `batchMint` with
   `count >= nudgeSize` and **`paymentAmount = 0`** — funded entirely by the outer batch's own in-flight
   balance (run-21 `M-01`'s mechanic: no lower bound on `paymentAmount`, allowance is
   `type(uint256).max`, mints spend the *contract's* balance). The inner frame therefore qualifies for
   the nudge without the attacker putting up a second batch's worth of money at the boundary; the real
   outlay is the `2 × nudgeSize` mints charged across the two frames.
3. The inner frame re-snapshots the nudge pot — now inflated by the outer batch's own donations — and is
   paid it at `:301`, to an attacker-controlled `recipient`.
4. The inner frame's own donations refill the pot, so the **outer** frame's `:301` transfer of the
   original pre-loop snapshot **also clears**. The pre-loop snapshot is paid **twice**, both times to
   attacker-controlled addresses.

**No third party names the attacker as anything. No integration is assumed. The value that moves is the
forward-donation pool** — i.e. value owed to future third-party claimants. This is the ground the finding
rests on, and it is why the C4 *"user input mistake"* known-invalid cannot reach it: **there is no second
party in the transaction to have erred.**

### Attack path B — caller-surplus theft (SECONDARY illustration: larger, silent, needs a payer)

The same missing guard also lets a hostile `recipient` rob the **payer** of the batch, which is the
larger single-transaction loss but does require someone else to fund the batch:

1. A batch is submitted with `recipient` set to a contract implementing `onERC1155Received`.
2. Control passes to it on an intermediate iteration, max allowance live, nudge snapshot unpaid.
3. The hook re-enters `batchMint` on the **deployed** contract, which has no `nonReentrant`.
4. The attacker fires on the **last** iteration specifically. The inner frame's free mint is charged to
   the contract (i.e. to the payer's in-flight money), then the inner frame runs step 10 and sweeps the
   residual to the **inner** `msg.sender`.
5. On the unwind the payment balance is `0`, the dust gate evaluates to `0`, and the outer refund branch
   is not taken. `totalPaid` still returns the full `paymentAmount`.

### Why the timing is load-bearing, not hand-waving

Firing early is **self-defeating, not merely weaker** — and this must not be presented as a mitigation.
The inner frame's `forceApprove(nftMinter, 0)` at `:290` runs on the unwind **before** the outer loop
resumes, so the outer's next `mint` pulls against a zero allowance and the entire transaction reverts
(the two `ERC20InsufficientAllowance` controls above). The attacker simply does not fire early, and
`count` is public calldata, so targeting the last iteration is free.

### Aggravating factors

- **Silent.** No revert, no event, and `totalPaid` returns the full `60_000_000`, affirmatively
  misreporting a clean, fully-spent batch.
- The refund is skipped **by the dust gate, not by an error**: the inner sweep leaves the balance at 0,
  so `remaining / DUST_THRESHOLD == 0`. The contract behaves exactly as written while the caller is robbed.
- **Frozen and deployed** — no in-place fix is possible.
- **The documented mitigation is CONFIRMED UNAVAILABLE.** Fresh read at block 25577241:
  `pauser() == address(0)` and `paused() == false` on **both** live instances
  (`0x81896F48…A1C7`, `0x86866e01…029d`). The contract's own *"pause first, then rescue"* sequence
  cannot be executed at all. Ledger `L-01` `919b71fd…` (*"neither live instance has a pauser"*) is
  **re-confirmed live**; the owed mainnet read has now been performed.

### Why Medium — and the single fact that would make it High

**Not a C4 known-invalid "user input mistake" — and path A settles this outright.** On path A the
attacker is caller *and* recipient and the value taken belongs to the forward-donation pool: **there is
no second party in the transaction, so there is no user input for anyone to have got wrong.** A finding
with a no-victim-required variant cannot be dismissed as victim error.

Path B survives the same test independently. Supplying a contract address to a parameter *designed to
accept contract addresses* is not a mistake but the documented use of the interface. `recipient` is an
explicit parameter, `recipient != msg.sender` is exercised by the project's own PoCs, and the ERC-1155
acceptance hook exists precisely so that vaults, custody multisigs, marketplace escrows and aggregators
can be recipients. The distinguishing test between user error and protocol defect is whether a careful,
non-negligent user can detect and avoid the harm — here they cannot: the transaction **succeeds**, no
event fires, and `totalPaid == paymentAmount` affirmatively reports a clean batch. A defect the victim
cannot observe after the fact is not a failure of victim diligence. The assumption is also **structural,
not a caller error**: the contract's own NatSpec at
[`:217-220`](https://github.com/Behodler/phoenix-nft-staking/blob/c881a428c87ef4ef42ba07a71be5d49101c9006d/src/BatchNFTMinter.sol#L217-L220)
**instructs callers to over-fund** because the per-mint price ramps.

**Medium is held on magnitude and attacker outlay — NOT on operational reachability.** Path A is
permissionless, needs no integration and no victim, so a reachability argument cannot bound it. What
bounds it is arithmetic:

- **Path A's extraction is capped by the pot** and costs the attacker a genuine `2 × nudgeSize` mint
  outlay. At live parameters on `0x86866e01…` — `nudgeSize = 40` at 15.857984 USDS/mint — that is
  **~1,268 USDS spent to net ~94.95 USDC**. The mechanic is real and permissionless; the extraction is a
  **value leak bounded by the pot**, not theft of an unbounded balance. That is a Medium, not a High.
- **Path B is the larger single-transaction loss** (the payer's entire surplus, silently) but is
  conditional on a third-party-recipient integration nobody has demonstrated: the PoC author searched for
  and found **no third-party-recipient batching integration in scope**. C4 High requires an attack path
  without hand-wavy hypotheticals, and *"assume someone integrates this way"* is exactly such a
  hypothetical.

## Recommended mitigation steps

### 1. There is no in-place fix — remediation is operational

`src/BatchNFTMinter.sol` is **frozen and deployed**. `nonReentrant` cannot be added to it. Until new
volume moves off it:

- **Constrain `recipient` at the front-end** to EOAs or to a vetted allowlist. ⚠ This closes **path B
  only** — an attacker calling the contract directly is not using the front-end, so path A is untouched
  by it.
- **The only operational control that reaches path A is the pot itself.** Keep the nudge pot small
  relative to `2 × nudgeSize × mintPrice`, or drain/disable the nudge on the frozen instances
  (`nudgePaymentToken = 0`, `nudgeSize = 0` — the same action already applied to `0x4ef0fDe4…` by
  `DisableNudgeAndDivertDonations`). This is the same unenforced funding relation carried at `L-04`.
- Route all new volume through the guarded `BatchNFTMinterMultiToken` once it is deployed and reviewed.

### 2. Set a non-zero `pauser` on both live instances

`0x81896F48…` and `0x86866e01…` both read `pauser() == address(0)`. Setting one is free and immediate,
and it is the only way the contract's own documented recovery sequence becomes executable at all. This
is joint with ledger `L-01` `919b71fd…` and with run-21 `M-01`'s third recommendation.

### 3. Answer the reachability question, in writing

Enumerate the callers of both live instances and record whether any passes a contract address as
`recipient`. ⚠ That fact decides whether **path B** is a live Medium or an urgent High. It **cannot**
lower this finding below Medium, because path A is permissionless and does not involve the front-end. It
is the same unowned-obligation shape already recorded against ledger `L-05` (`990d8c37…`).

### 4. Do not read the twin's guard as closing this

The omission did not relocate — it **stayed behind** on the deployed file. A fingerprint reconciliation
that reads the new file's `nonReentrant` as *"ledger L-01 fixed"* would be wrong.
