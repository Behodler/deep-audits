## Finding description and impact

### Summary

`batchMint` places **no lower bound on `paymentAmount`**, grants the minter a `type(uint256).max`
allowance regardless of what the caller actually pulled in, mints out of the **contract's** payment-token
balance, and then sweeps the **whole remaining payment-token balance** to `msg.sender` with no
qualification check. A caller passing `paymentAmount = 0` therefore mints NFTs paid for out of value
somebody else put into the contract, and walks away with the remainder — total outlay: gas only.

This is the **composition** of three separately-filed legs (the un-gated step-10 sweep, ledger `M-01`
`fcaca002…`; the uncapped `forceApprove`, ledger `M-07` `ad36260f…`; and the absent `paymentAmount`
floor). It must **not** be closed into either component: closing it into one buries the composition,
which is what refutes run-20's severity bound.

`totalPaid`'s floor at `0` is the contract itself admitting the refund can exceed the contribution.

### Attack path

1. Attacker observes a batch minter holding payment token `R > 0`. This is a **designed** operating
   state, not an accident: the contract's own NatSpec at
   [`BatchNFTMinter.sol#L63-L68`](https://github.com/Behodler/phoenix-nft-staking/blob/c881a428c87ef4ef42ba07a71be5d49101c9006d/src/BatchNFTMinter.sol#L63-L68)
   documents the residual as *"intentionally left … picked up by the next batch's sweep"* and a donator as one who
   *"simply donates to the next caller"*, and the live `NudgeRatchet` wiring forwards 100 % of every mint price back in.
2. Attacker calls `batchMint(count, recipient = attacker, paymentAmount = 0, …)`. No allowance, no
   balance, no privileged role, no unusual token behaviour.
3. `:283`/`:357` pulls 0 (both USDC and USDS accept a zero-value `transferFrom`). `:284`/`:360` grants a
   `type(uint256).max` allowance — **not capped at `paymentAmount`**.
4. The mint loop spends the **contract's** balance for `count` NFTs.
5. `:305-307`/`:381-383` sweeps the whole remaining balance to `msg.sender`, ungated by `nudgeSize`,
   bounded below only by `DUST_THRESHOLD = 1e6`.

### Present exposure — the attacker cannot arm this, and no live route arms it

> The bound stated here is deliberately **not** "the balance happens to be zero on the day we looked".
> TVL at audit time does not bound severity, and this finding does not ask it to. The two load-bearing
> grounds are (1) the precondition is **not attacker-creatable** and (2) **no live inflow route delivers
> the payment token**. The measured balance is corroboration for (2), nothing more.

**Ground 1 — the attacker cannot profitably create the precondition.** This is the test that decides
whether the funding requirement is a genuine external requirement or an attacker-controlled formality:

- **Self-funding is net-neutral.** Donate `R`, then call `batchMint(paymentAmount = 0)` — you recover
  your own `R`. Nothing is extracted.
- **The compound case is loss-making.** Donate ~634 USDS to `0x86866e01…`, then call `count = 40,
  paymentAmount = 0`: you take back the USDS *and* the 94.95 USDC nudge pot, but you paid ~634 USDS for
  40 NFTs. That is the **designed nudge bounty operating as intended**, not a drain
  (`mainnet-verification-ECON-001.md` §5).
- **Dust accumulation cannot arm the free-mint leg.** Residue below `DUST_THRESHOLD = 1e6` is retained,
  and the next sweep at `>= 1e6` hands it to the next honest caller. On `0x81896F48…` (70.000000
  USDC/mint) the retainable residue is `< 1 USDC` — two orders of magnitude short of funding a single
  free mint.

Arming therefore requires a **third party or the owner** to route payment token in. That is
attacker-uncontrolled, and it is the C4 Medium external requirement.

**Ground 2 — no live inflow route delivers the payment token to either instance.** All three routes into
`0x86866e01…` — `BalancerPoolerV2`'s PSM `buyGem`, the SYA `nudgeSplit = 30`, and the `NudgeRatchet`
forward — deliver **USDC**, which is that instance's *nudge* token, not its payment token (USDS). Nothing
delivers USDC into `0x81896F48…`, the instance whose payment token **is** USDC
(`mainnet-verification-ECON-001.md` §5). This is a statement about the system **as wired**, not about a
balance.

**Ground 3 (corroboration only) — and consistent with the above, the measured balance is zero.** Fresh
read-only mainnet verification, chainid 1, **block 25577241**, RPC liveness probe OK
(`mainnet-verification-ECON-001.md`):

| `0x81896F48…A1C7` (RatchetBatchNFTMinter, UI entrypoint, dispatcher idx 7) | **USDC** | 70.000000 USDC | USDS | **0** |

- **Both live instances hold 0 of their payment token**, and 0 of every other probed ERC20 (USDC, USDS,
  PhUSD, sUSDS, Flax, AutoUSDC) and 0 ETH.
- The zero is **stable across the sampled history**: flat 0 at blocks 25400000, 25450000, 25500000,

### Why Medium and not High

The two arguments do not collide. The High case establishes that the **mechanic** is unbounded and
unprivileged — PoC-proven 11/11 on both files including the deployed one, attacker outlay gas only.

The Medium case rests on the **funding precondition being a genuine external requirement**, in the C4
sense: the attacker cannot profitably create it (Ground 1 — self-funding is net-neutral, the compound
case is loss-making, dust cannot accumulate to it), and no live route in the system as wired delivers the
payment token to either instance (Ground 2). A precondition an attacker can create for themselves would
*not* cap severity at Medium; this one they cannot. The measured zero balance is corroboration of Ground
2, **not the argument itself** — a balance-at-audit-time census would be the weakest available version of
this bound and is deliberately not the version relied on.

**The ground for holding this at Medium is explicitly NOT "the multi-token file is not deployed"** —
run-20 R-6 forbids that ground and it is rejected here. The mechanic is byte-identical and PoC-proven on
the file that **is** deployed.

C4 Medium fit: a value leak with stated assumptions (`R >= C`) and stated **external requirements**
(funding), on unpatched live code with no pause and no reliable rescue.

## Recommended mitigation steps

### 1. Cap the approval at `paymentAmount` — the structural fix

`src/BatchNFTMinter.sol:284` / `src/BatchNFTMinterMultiToken.sol:360`:

```diff
-        paymentToken.forceApprove(address(nftMinter), type(uint256).max);
+        // Never let the minter spend more than this caller actually pulled in.
+        paymentToken.forceApprove(address(nftMinter), paymentAmount);
```

This alone kills the mechanic **structurally rather than balance-dependently**: at `paymentAmount = 0`
the mint reverts at the allowance boundary regardless of the contract's balance.

### 2. Establish and test `refund <= paymentAmount`, always

Bound the step-10 refund by the caller's own pull rather than by a post-hoc whole-balance read. Per
run-20 D-35: **establish the property and test it — do not ship an unvalidated patch.** No fix is
proposed-and-validated by this run's PoC; the recommendation is reasoned, not validated.

### 3. Set a non-zero `pauser` on both live instances

`0x81896F48…` and `0x86866e01…` currently have `pauser() == address(0)`. Setting one is free, immediate,
and restores the only recovery sequence the contract's own documentation considers dependable.

### 4. Ledger hygiene — do not collapse

- Re-anchor ledger `M-01` `fcaca002…` (step-10 sweep, open) and ledger `M-07` `ad36260f…`
  (`forceApprove` max, open) to `src/BatchNFTMinterMultiToken.sol` — their fingerprints will not match at
  HEAD after the story-022 Stage 7 relocation.
- **Do not auto-close this finding into either component.** It is the *composition* of both plus a new
  zero-`paymentAmount` entry vector; closing it into either buries the composition and restores a bound
  that has been refuted.
- Do **not** collapse this with M-02 (`c847207d…`). M-02 is the reentrancy composition with a **distinct
  victim** — the outer caller — and it is not bounded by the `R = 0` mainnet mitigation that holds this
  finding at Medium.
