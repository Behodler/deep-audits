claims; story-022 conforms on 11 / 11. The `diff` work, the step-by-step check of the normative

The problems this run found are **in the stories themselves**, not in the code's fidelity to them:

1. **One unsafe normative clause.** `docs/multi-token-nudge.md` §4.5 asserts a safety property

Law 1 overrides Law 2: where a story's own intended behaviour introduces an exploit or a value leak,
the **story** is flagged — a faithful-but-exploitable implementation is not blessed. Both of the above

## F-20-01 — §4.5's "duplicates fail closed" is false under §4.2's own donation premise

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Type** | story-unsafe (**security escalation** — Law 1 overrides Law 2) |
| **Severity** | potential-medium |
| **Story** | `story-022` |
| **File:line** | `src/BatchNFTMinter.sol:429` (snapshot), `:452-461` (payout); range `416-461` |
| **Security cross-ref** | **DEDUP-20-001** — *"Duplicate `rewardTokens` entries pay the same pre-loop snapshot k times and recapture the batch's own donations"* (Medium) |

### The spec contradicts itself — both clauses quoted verbatim

> **`docs/multi-token-nudge.md` §4.2 — "Snapshot BEFORE the mint loop — LOAD-BEARING, DO NOT 'SIMPLIFY'"**
>
> "The dispatcher donates reward token into this contract on every mint. Reading
> balances *before* the loop means the batcher is paid only the **prior
> accumulated pot**; the donations generated by their own batch stay behind to
> seed the next claimant. This is the "donate forward" mechanic, and it is the
> only thing preventing a caller from **funding their own reward within a single
> transaction** — a post-loop read would refund a batch's own donations straight
> back to the batcher, collapsing the incentive to a no-op round-trip."

> **`docs/multi-token-nudge.md` §4.5 — "Malformed arrays are the caller's problem"**
>
> "- **Duplicates** (`[USDC, USDC]`): both entries snapshot the same balance. The
> first transfer drains it; the second either reverts on insufficient balance
> or transfers into a now-empty pot. Either way it fails closed and harms only
> the caller. No dedupe pass — that is O(n²) gas charged to every honest caller
> to protect one careless one."

The commit body restates the unsafe clause as a design decision: *"4.5 no dedupe pass; duplicates fail closed"* (`2bf13cb`).

§4.5's reasoning silently assumes the balance at payout equals the balance at snapshot. **§4.2 says it
does not** — the gap is exactly the batch's own donations `D`.

### Actual behaviour at HEAD

The snapshot pass records `snapshot[i] = balanceOf(this)` for **every** entry, with no dedupe
(`src/BatchNFTMinter.sol:429`):

```solidity
uint256 available = qualifies ? IERC20(rewardToken).balanceOf(address(this)) : 0;
```

The payout pass then transfers each entry unconditionally (`:452-461`):

```solidity
for (uint256 i; i < tokenCount; ++i) {
    uint256 amount = snapshot[i];
    if (amount == 0) continue;
    address rewardToken = rewardTokens[i];
    IERC20(rewardToken).safeTransfer(recipient, amount);
    emit NudgePaid(recipient, rewardToken, amount);
}
```

With pre-loop pot `B`, own-batch donations `D`, and `k` duplicate entries, the payout pass transfers
`k·B` against a live balance of `B + D` and **succeeds whenever `k·B ≤ B + D`**. The caller recaptures
up to 100% of their own donations at **zero marginal mint cost** relative to an honest single-entry
claim — i.e. the duplicate array strictly dominates honest behaviour for every claimant, and the
standing donate-forward pot degenerates to nothing. That is precisely the "self-funded round-trip"
§4.2 declares load-bearing to prevent.

**Production-live, not mock-only.** The pinned `DISPATCHER_INDEX = 4` is `BalancerPoolerV2`, whose
`primeToken()` is USDS while it forwards a `donationSplit`% **USDC** cut to `batchMinter` on every
dispatch. USDC is therefore simultaneously a legal reward token (the §4.1 exclusion does not fire) and
a per-mint donation — the exact configuration the PoC models.

### PoC evidence

`workspace/phoenix-nft-staking/test/PoC_DuplicateRewardWithDonations.t.sol` — **3/3 PASS**:

- `test_DuplicateDoesNotFailClosedWhenDispatcherDonates` — `B = 3e6`, `D = 15e6`, 2 entries →
  recipient receives `2·B`; contract left with `D − B` instead of `D`.
- `test_ManyDuplicatesDrainOwnDonationsEntirely` — 6 entries → contract balance **0**; the batcher
  takes `priorPot + all of its own donations`. Nothing remains to seed the next claimant.

Corroborated independently by `InvariantBatchNudge.t.sol` (`invariant_nudgeSolvency`:
`paid=6000000 prePot=3000000 count=8 listLen=3` — paid 2× the snapshot pot; `invariant_nudgeNoSelfFund`:
`excess=21000000`), each shrunk to a **1-call** counterexample, with `test_coverageCensus` proving
non-vacuity (234 successful `batchMint` calls, 69 qualified, 61 with a reward token actually moved,
112 duplicate lists).

**Honest bound on magnitude** (`PoC_EconRatchetSweepAndDuplicates.t.sol::PoC_DuplicateMagnitudeMainnetShape`,
3/3 PASS): at the *observed mainnet shape* `test_AtObservedShape_DuplicateStillFailsClosed` — the
duplicate path does fail closed. At a large-batch shape, `test_LargeBatchRecapturesItsOwnDonation`
measures donation 776.40 USDC with **704.53 recaptured** (≈90.7% self-recapture), and
`test_BackToBackSteadyState_DonateForwardNullified` measures prior pot 77.64 USDC, paid out 155.28,
**0 left behind**. Both bounds must travel with the finding.

**A second unenforced clause in the same lineage.** The contract's honeypot rebuttal
(`src/BatchNFTMinter.sol:51-61`) asserts that *"the pot is **by construction** a fraction of the cost of
the `nudgeSize` mints required to qualify — every claim is net-positive for the protocol."* **Nothing in
`batchMint` enforces that bound.** The eligibility gate (`:349-354`) compares a **count to a count**, and
the payout (`:429`, `:452-461`) is the entire snapshotted balance, so a 1× payer and a 1000× payer receive
byte-identical payouts. The re-authored lineage PoCs execute the counterexample: cost to qualify
5,256.33 vs pot 50,000 captured (**≈9.5×**, `test_PoC_H01_PotIsNotBoundedByMintCost`), and **20,000×** at
a modest pinned price (`test_NudgeDrain_modestPrice_stillNetProfitable`). This is the same failure shape
as §4.5 — a normative safety claim stated as structural ("by construction", "fails closed") that no code
backs — and it sets the ceiling on F-20-01's magnitude: the pot the duplicate path recaptures is itself
unbounded relative to mint cost. See "Nudge-lineage verification debt DISCHARGED" in the
cross-references section below.

### ⚠ FIX TRAP — recorded per D-16

**The obvious remedy is the one the spec explicitly warns against.** Re-reading `balanceOf` at the
payout site is *exactly* the §4.2 refactor trap ("a post-loop read would refund a batch's own donations
straight back to the batcher"), and the contract carries a do-not-simplify comment at three sites
(`:327-344`, `:392-399`, `:440-447`) saying so. Applying it would hand **every** batcher their full
donation back — a strictly worse outcome than the bug being fixed.

A correct fix must **clamp** or **dedupe**:

- pay `min(snapshot[i], balanceOf(this))` while zeroing subsequent duplicate entries, **or**
- accumulate per-token amounts into a deduped payout map, **or**
- cap each payout at the live balance and skip on shortfall.

**Evaluate jointly with the fee-on-transfer gap.** Pattern-matcher notes that clamping to the delivered
/ live amount also closes the §4.4 payout-vs-delivered discrepancy (DEDUP-20-006, QA; kept at QA per
D-19). The two remedies overlap and should be designed together rather than in two passes.

**Doc obligation.** Whichever remedy lands, §4.5 must be rewritten — the clause as written is the
defect — and `test_DuplicateRewardTokenFailsClosed` must be re-armed with a donating dispatcher
(see F-20-02).

## F-20-03 — `NFTStakerDepletion.depositFor` still pays the migrator: the fix was never back-ported

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Type** | invariant-violation (**security escalation** — Law 1 overrides Law 2) |
| **Severity** | potential-medium (severity dispute recorded: Medium vs plausible High) |
| **Story** | `story-021` |
| **File:line** | `src/NFTStakerDepletion.sol:756` (range `748-758`) |
| **Security cross-ref** | **DEDUP-20-009** (Medium — *"`NFTStakerDepletion.depositFor` settles a staker's pending phUSD with `_safePay`, paying the MIGRATOR while emitting `Claimed(user, …)`"*); **DEDUP-20-010** (Low — *"`NFTStakerMigrator` has no rescue function of any kind, no fallback and no receive"*) |

### The story names the bug — verbatim

> **`story-021` commit body (`f65aec9`):**
>
> "- depositFor settles existing pending via _safePayTo(user, ...) rather than
>   _safePay(...), which would pay the migrator"

> **`src/NFTStakerPriceScaledMigrateReady.sol:869-876` (ported NatSpec):**
>
> "DELTA vs `NFTStakerDepletion.depositFor`: the pre-credit
> settlement pays via `_safePayTo(user, ...)` rather than
> `_safePay(...)`. `msg.sender` here is the MIGRATOR, so the
> `_safePay` form would route an existing staker's earned phUSD to
> the orchestrator while emitting `Claimed(user, ...)`. Only
> reachable when the deposit target already holds a position (a
> partial `InPlaceNFTStakerMigrator` round-trip, or migrating into
> a staker the user is already in), **but wrong in every case**."

And, in the same commit body, the scope declaration that makes the omission permanent:

> "NFTStaker.sol, NFTStakerPriceScaled.sol, NFTStakerDepletion.sol, both
> migrators, INFTStakerMigratable and all existing tests are untouched"

That last claim is **literally true** — `git show --stat f65aec9` lists exactly three files, all
additions (`src/NFTStakerPriceScaledMigrateReady.sol` +1005, `test/NFTStakerPriceScaledMigrateReady.t.sol`
+717, `test/NFTStakerPriceScaledMigratorOrchestrators.t.sol` +399). **Being literally true is the defect.**

### Actual behaviour at HEAD

The fix landed **only** in the new fourth copy. The original — the contract actually paired with the
two live migrators — retains the `_safePay` form (`src/NFTStakerDepletion.sol:748-758`):

```solidity
function depositFor(address user, uint256 amount) external override nonReentrant onlyMigrator {
    require(amount > 0, "NFTStaker: zero deposit");
    require(poolState == PoolState.Active, "NFTStaker: not active");
    _syncBudget();
    UserInfo storage info = users[user];
    if (info.amount > 0) {
        uint256 pending = (info.amount * accRewardPerShare) / ACC_PRECISION - info.rewardDebt;
        if (pending > 0) {
            pending = _safePay(pending);                  // <-- pays msg.sender == the MIGRATOR
            if (pending > 0) emit Claimed(user, pending); // <-- event names the USER
        }
    }
```

`_safePay` delegates to `_safePayTo(msg.sender, …)` (`src/NFTStakerDepletion.sol:593-595`), and under
`onlyMigrator` `msg.sender` is the orchestrator contract. The staker's `committedDebt` is decremented as
though the user was paid, and a false `Claimed(user, …)` event is emitted — misleading indexers and any
off-chain reconciliation.

Compare `src/NFTStakerPriceScaledMigrateReady.sol:888`, which is correct: `pending = _safePayTo(user, pending);`

**Reachability.** `InPlaceNFTStakerMigrator.migrateIn` (`src/InPlaceNFTStakerMigrator.sol:226`) and
`NFTStakerMigrator.migrate` (`src/NFTStakerMigrator.sol:102`) both call `depositFor`. The
`info.amount > 0` branch fires whenever the deposit target already holds a position — e.g. a parked user
who re-staked directly between `finalizeAndReset` and `migrateIn`, or a user migrating into a staker
they are already in.

**Recovery is asymmetric, and that asymmetry drives severity:**

- `InPlaceNFTStakerMigrator` has an unconditional owner `rescueERC20` (`:268`) → **recoverable**.
- `NFTStakerMigrator` has **no rescue function at all** — its only externals are `initiateMigration`
  and `migrate` → phUSD routed there is **permanently stranded** (filed separately as DEDUP-20-010).

### PoC evidence

`workspace/phoenix-nft-staking/test/PoC_Drift01_DepositForPaysMigrator.t.sol` — **4/4 PASS**, the
strongest PoC in the run:

| test | pending owed | user received | routed to migrator | recoverable? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `testA_DepositForPaysMigratorNotUser` | 82,191.780821…e18 | **0** | 82,191.780821…e18 | — |
| `testC_InPlaceMigrateInCapturesReward` | 8,219.178…e18 | **0** | 8,219.178…e18 | **yes** (`rescueERC20`) |
| `testD_CrossStakerMigrateStrandsRewardPermanently` | 82,191.78…e18 | net 82,191.78…e18 | 82,191.78…e18 | **NO — no rescue fn** |
| `testB_BatchMigratePaysUserCorrectly` | — | correct | — | control (path is clean) |

`testB` is a genuine control: the `batchMigrate` path pays the user correctly, so the defect is
path-specific rather than a blanket claim.

Note the negative result that must travel with this finding: `InvariantForkParity.t.sol` passes 3/3
across 128,000 calls, but it compares **copy #2 against copy #4 only** and exposes no migration surface
— **it does not test this drift at all**. A green fork-parity harness is not evidence that fork drift is
absent.

### Fix

One line, at `src/NFTStakerDepletion.sol:756`:

```solidity
pending = _safePayTo(user, pending);
```

This is a **security finding, not a spec note** — it is a live misdirection of user funds in an
in-scope contract, with an unrecoverable path.

> **`src/NFTStakerPriceScaledMigrateReady.sol:36-40`:** "MAINTENANCE COUPLING: this is a

### Actual behaviour at HEAD

The repo now carries **four** hand-maintained ~1000-line stakers with no shared base: `NFTStaker`,
`NFTStakerPriceScaled`, `NFTStakerDepletion`, `NFTStakerPriceScaledMigrateReady`.

**F-20-03 is the first materialised instance of the hazard — and it materialised inside the very commit
that created the fourth clone**, in the *reverse* direction from the one the watch note anticipated.
`WATCH-17-maintenance-coupling-drift` was framed as "a future fix to `NFTStaker` won't propagate to the
clones". What actually happened is the mirror image: a fix was authored **in the clone** and never
back-ported to the original that is wired to the live migrators.

Under **Law 3** the no-shared-base design decision is the owner's and is not re-litigated here. What
*is* in scope is the non-obvious consequence: a competent, non-malicious maintainer fixing
`NFTStakerDepletion` has no signal that the correct code already exists ~200 lines away in a different
file. Surprise ⇒ footgun ⇒ report.

### Obligations carried forward

- Widen `WATCH-17-maintenance-coupling-drift` from three clones to **four** (per D-05), and promote it
  from watch-note to **active hazard with a demonstrated instance**.
- Make the next run's pairwise diff cover **all four** stakers, not the two the run-18 note names.
- Land F-20-03 as the concrete back-port.

### ⚠ STANDING DIRECTION RULE — must survive this run

> **story-020's depletion rate/window fix must NOT be mirrored into the APY/runway copies.**

The fork copies are **intentionally divergent** on the depletion schedule. A well-meaning "sync the
clones" pass — the natural over-correction to F-20-03/F-20-04 — would break them. The rate model
differs by design: in `NFTStakerDepletion` the rate is `budget / window` and therefore
`totalStaked`-independent, so the `depositFor` tail recompute there is **cosmetic**; on the
price-scaled copies `R = S · targetAPY / SECONDS_PER_YEAR` with `S = totalStaked · latestPrice`, so the
same tail is **load-bearing** (`src/NFTStakerPriceScaledMigrateReady.sol:901`). Mirroring the depletion
fix into the APY copies would target the wrong invariant.

**Back-port only the specific, identified delta (F-20-03's one line). Do not mechanically converge the
four files.**

## F-20-05 — §4.1's threat model overstates what the guard protects (spec prose)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Type** | faithfulness (spec-prose deviation; **no code change required**) |
| **Severity** | low |
| **Story** | `story-022` |
| **File:line** | `src/BatchNFTMinter.sol:381` (range `380-387`) |
| **Security cross-ref** | **DEDUP-20-002** (Medium — ECON-001, *"Step-10 sweep transfers the contract's ENTIRE payment-token balance to `msg.sender` with no `nudgeSize` or `qualifies` gate"*); DEDUP-20-004 (QA — *"`totalPaid` floors at 0 on a net-positive call"*) |

### Spec text (verbatim)

> **`docs/multi-token-nudge.md` §4.1 — "The payment token can never be a reward token — LOAD-BEARING"**
>
> "**It is now an untrusted-input check on a
> value an attacker fully controls, and it is the thing standing between a caller
> and the payment-token balance held mid-transaction.**
>
> Two distinct failures it prevents:
> - claiming the contract's payment-token balance (accumulated sub-threshold dust
>   from prior batches) as "reward";
> - perturbing the snapshot/refund accounting, since step 5's pull and step 10's
>   sweep both operate on `paymentToken`."

### Actual behaviour at HEAD

The §4.1 guard itself is **correctly and unconditionally implemented**
(`src/BatchNFTMinter.sol:426-428`, per-element, ahead of the `qualifies ? balanceOf : 0` ternary and
ahead of any fund movement), and its **narrow** claim about *sub-threshold* dust holds
(`remaining / DUST_THRESHOLD == 0` leaves it in place).

The **broader** framing does not. Step 10's dust sweep (`:380-387`) already transfers the contract's
**entire** payment-token balance to `msg.sender` whenever it is ≥ `DUST_THRESHOLD` (1e6 wei — 1e-12 of
an 18dp USDS), on a `count == 1` call with empty reward arrays, with **no `nudgeSize` or `qualifies`
qualification at all**:

```solidity
// --- 10. Dust sweep of residual payment token back to msg.sender. ---
uint256 remaining = paymentToken.balanceOf(address(this));
if (remaining / DUST_THRESHOLD != 0) {
    paymentToken.safeTransfer(msg.sender, remaining);
    totalPaid = paymentAmount > remaining ? paymentAmount - remaining : 0;
} else {
    totalPaid = paymentAmount;
}
```

So the §4.1 guard is not "the thing standing between a caller and the payment-token balance" — the
sweep sits right next to it and is wide open.

### PoC evidence

- `workspace/phoenix-nft-staking/test/PoC_DuplicateRewardWithDonations.t.sol::PoC_PaymentTokenSweep`
  — `test_AnyoneSweepsPaymentTokenBalanceWithoutQualifying` (**PASS**): 500 USDS donated to the contract
  is swept in full to a **non-qualifying** caller, and `batchMint` returns `totalPaid == 0` while the
  caller is net positive.
- `PoC_EconRatchetSweepAndDuplicates.t.sol::PoC_RatchetPrimeTokenSweep` (**3/3 PASS**):
  `test_CountOneCallerSweepsEntireRatchetPot`; `test_QualifyingBatcherCannotListThePotAsset` — the crux
  (the §4.1 guard blocks the *legitimate* claimer while leaving the sweep open);
  `test_RatchetBatchMintIsFree`.
- `InvariantBatchNudge.t.sol::invariant_sweep` — **BROKEN**, 1-call counterexample
  (`caller net gain = 11263884099603271484375`).
- `MedusaNudge.sol` — independent, differently-engined reproduction of INV-SWEEP in a **single call**
  from a cold corpus (26 pass / 1 fail).

### Disposition

**This is not a code deviation.** The sweep is pre-existing (byte-identical since `ab07199 [story-013]`,
2026-05-29), is explicitly preserved by §4.6 ("`DUST_THRESHOLD` sweep semantics and the `totalPaid`
return value"), and is documented in the contract itself (`src/BatchNFTMinter.sol:68` — "A griefer who
pre-deposits payment-token to this contract simply donates to the next caller"). The **implementation is
faithful**; the normative doc's *justification* is what is wrong.

## F-20-06 — removing the deploy-time collision guard is not equivalent to the on-chain check

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Type** | story-unsafe (Law-3 operator footgun) |
| **Severity** | low |
| **Story** | `story-022` |
| **File:line** | `script/DeployBatchNFTMinter.s.sol:84` (range `82-90`) |
| **Security cross-ref** | DEDUP-20-003 (Low) — *"Nothing ties `dispatcherIndex`'s primeToken to the contract's funding assets, and the removed deploy-time guard was not equivalently replaced"* |

### Spec text (verbatim)

> **`story-022` commit body (`2bf13cb`):** "Deploy script (stage 5): drops setNudgePaymentToken, the
> nudgePaymentToken() post-condition, the USDC constant/log and the meaningless USDC != TOKEN_MINTER
> require; prose revised, keeping the SYA.setNudgeAddress 30% drip context."

> **`script/DeployBatchNFTMinter.s.sol:39-42`:** "The old
> "nudge token == payment token" deploy guard is gone: that
> collision is now checked ON-CHAIN, per call, against every element
> of the caller's `rewardTokens` array
> (`BatchMint__RewardTokenIsPaymentToken`)."

### Actual behaviour at HEAD

The removal is faithful to the commit message — every listed item is gone, and the replacement comment
sits at `script/DeployBatchNFTMinter.s.sol:84-87`:

```solidity
// No reward-token guard here any more: the reward asset is not deploy
// configuration. The payment-token collision that used to be an
// operator concern is enforced on-chain on every `batchMint` call,
// against every element of the caller-supplied `rewardTokens` array.
```

But the stated **equivalence does not hold** for the failure mode the old guard existed to catch. The
on-chain check reverts the **caller**; it does not warn the **operator**. If `DISPATCHER_INDEX` is
pointed at a dispatcher whose `primeToken()` equals the token that same dispatcher donates to
`batchMinter`, the nudge is **structurally dead**: every attempt to claim it reverts
`BatchMint__RewardTokenIsPaymentToken`, and the accumulated pot is instead swept to whichever caller
next runs a 1-mint batch (F-20-05 / DEDUP-20-002).

**Not hypothetical.** In the sibling repo:

- `yield-claim-nft/src/dispatchers/PromotionUniV2_Eth.sol` has `primeToken() == USDC` (`:189-191`)
  **and** donates USDC to `batchMinter` (`:340`);
- `NudgeRatchet.sol` likewise forwards USDC to `batchMinter` under a 6-decimal deploy guard.

Only the currently-pinned `BalancerPoolerV2` (index 4: `primeToken` USDS, donates USDC) is safe — and
the script's own NatSpec flags `DISPATCHER_INDEX = 4` as "ASSUMED … the operator MUST confirm", i.e. a
repoint is an **expected** operation. (D-17's live mainnet reads found the collision already realised on
`RatchetBatchNFTMinter` `0x81896f48…`, where payment token and donated asset are both USDC.)

**Law-3 test:** would a competent, non-malicious operator repointing `DISPATCHER_INDEX` be surprised
that the nudge silently becomes unclaimable and leaks? **Yes ⇒ footgun ⇒ in scope** as an operational
hazard, not suppressed as a reckless-admin issue.

### Remedy

Restore an **operator-facing** guard in the deploy script (and/or a `setDispatcherIndex` sanity log):
resolve `configs(DISPATCHER_INDEX).dispatcher.primeToken()` and assert it differs from the intended
reward asset for that dispatcher. Cheap, deploy-time only, and it restores the operator signal the
on-chain check structurally cannot give.

This also narrows — but does not close — ledger **L-04**: the hardcoded-USDC half is gone; the hardcoded
`DISPATCHER_INDEX = 4` half remains with only a prose "operator MUST confirm".
