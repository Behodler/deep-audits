## Finding description and impact

### Summary

`userMigrate` is permissionless by design — it is the frozen-state escape hatch that lets a user exit a staker the operator has frozen ([`NFTStakerPriceScaledMigrateReady.sol#L827`](https://github.com/Behodler/phoenix-nft-staking/blob/main/src/NFTStakerPriceScaledMigrateReady.sol#L827)). Because the target staker is Active and accruing while the operator's batch migration is still in progress, a user who is watching the chain can self-exit the frozen source and immediately re-stake into the target, jumping the operator's queue and accruing from the shared reward budget for the entire operator lag. Users who wait to be migrated accrue nothing over that window.

### Vulnerability details

1. The operator initiates the migration; the source staker freezes. `userMigrate` remains callable — deliberately, and correctly.
2. A watching user calls `userMigrate` to self-exit the frozen source.
3. In the same block they stake into the Active target.
4. They accrue from the shared budget for the whole operator lag. Queued users accrue nothing until the operator's slice reaches them.

There is no privilege requirement, no external actor, and no timing subtlety beyond watching for the migration to begin. The cost is two transactions. The precondition is simply that `targetAPY` is non-zero during the operator lag — which it is, by default.

### Impact

Sophisticated users systematically out-earn passive users across every migration, out of a **shared, fixed** reward budget: the advantage is not created, it is taken from the users who waited.

Measured at a 5-day operator lag (`test_ECON_H`): Alice, self-advancing, receives **43.151e18 phUSD**; Bob, waiting for the operator, receives **36.986e18** — a **1666 bps** edge.

### Severity rationale — why this was raised from Low

The finding was filed **Low** on an agency argument: `userMigrate` is permissionless and symmetric, so any user can protect themselves with two transactions, whereas in M-04 (`a0967cce…`) the harmed user has no agency at all because the operator assigns the slice.

The severity-auditor demonstrated that the Low was **understated**, and the orchestrator raised it to **Medium** (ruling R-1). The reasoning:

- **The victim and the loss are identical across both findings.** Bob receives **36.986e18** in `test_ECON_A` (M-04, filed Medium) and **36.986e18** in `test_ECON_H` (this finding, filed Low) — same victim, same loss, same shared budget. Only the *beneficiary* differs.
- **This finding's edge is the larger of the two**: 1666 bps here versus 1000 bps in M-04.
- **C4 severity is assessed on impact.** The agency argument describes a mitigation available to a hypothetical *attentive* user; it is not a bound on the harm suffered by the *actual* victim. Every MEV finding in DeFi could be argued away on the premise that the loser could have been faster.

Two findings with an identical measured victim loss should not straddle a severity boundary on the basis of who benefits.

### Relationship to M-04 — merge proposed, merge rejected

The severity-auditor's preferred disposition was to **merge** this finding with M-04 (`a0967cce…`) into a single Medium, framed as *"emissions run during a staggered migration window; time-in-pool is set by slice assignment or by self-advancing, not by entitlement"* — two actors, two measured magnitudes, one defect — on the grounds that two exploitation shapes of one defect should not straddle a severity boundary, and that merging avoids inflating the Medium count.

**The merge was rejected** under the dedup `doNotCollapseRegister` (D-24), which forbids exactly this pairing:

- **Different actor** — user self-advance versus operator slice assignment.
- **Different mechanism** — permissionless self-exit versus operator-controlled ordering.
- **Decisively: fixing one does not fix the other.** Gating `userMigrate` closes this finding and leaves M-04 fully open; fixing slice ordering closes M-04 and leaves self-advance fully open.

Merging would let a fix for one read as closing both. A shared remedy is not a shared root cause. Two Mediums, each with its own remediation.

> **Cross-reference (reciprocal, required).** Related mechanism, **independent fix required** — see **M-04** (`a0967cceac39…`, `InPlaceNFTStakerMigrator.migrateIn`, operator-assigned slice ordering, 1000 bps).

### Honest bounds

- No principal is at risk; the harm is a redistribution of accrued yield within a fixed shared budget.
- The magnitude scales with the operator lag. The measured 1666 bps assumes a 5-day lag; a short, automated migration window shrinks it proportionally.
- The precondition is `targetAPY` non-zero during the lag. There are no external requirements.

## Recommended mitigation steps

Set `targetAPY` to **0** between `finalizeAndReset` and the final migration slice, so that no economic weight attaches to who lands in the target first:

```solidity
// On entering the migration window, before the first slice:
targetStaker.setAPY(0);
// ... operator slices, and any permissionless userMigrate calls, land here ...
// After the final slice has been migrated in:
targetStaker.setAPY(intendedAPY);
```

Two constraints on the fix:

1. **Do not remove `userMigrate`'s permissionlessness.** It is a load-bearing frozen-state escape hatch; gating it would trade an economic redistribution for a fund-freeze hazard.
2. **This remedy also happens to close M-04, but that is a property of this particular remedy, not of the defects.** On any other remediation path the two require independent fixes — for example, gating `userMigrate` closes this finding and leaves M-04 fully open. Do not treat a fix for either finding as evidence that the other is closed; verify both against their own PoCs (`test_ECON_H` and `test_ECON_A`).
