## Owner's reason (verbatim)

> The yield strategies enforce a max-slippage setting. ERC4626MarketYieldStrategy computes
> minOut = idealUnderlying * (MAX_BPS - slippageToleranceBps) / MAX_BPS
> (ERC4626MarketYieldStrategy.sol:166-168) and reverts below it, so the per-call haircut — and
> therefore the protocol's per-call exposure — is capped proportionally at whatever tolerance is
> configured. At the live USDe setting of 120 bps that ceilings the leak at 1.2% of any single exit,
> and the fork PoC's bisected breakeven for a third-party attacker is 142 bps, so no profitable
> third-party sandwich exists at the configured tolerance. Accepted as a residual, owner-managed
> operational risk.

## Caveats carried with the decision

These were verified by the auditor and travel **with** the closure so its limits are not lost. They are
**not** a re-litigation of the owner's decision.

1. **The cap is proportional, not absolute.** The setter's only code bound is
   `require(_bps <= MAX_BPS)` at `ERC4626MarketYieldStrategy.sol:88-89` — i.e. **100%**. The ceiling is
   whatever tolerance is configured; it is not a safety bound in itself.
2. **The mainnet fleet is internally inconsistent on this parameter.**
   `phoenix-phase-2-staging` `script/MigrateStableStakerMainnet.s.sol:312` enforces a preflight band of
   `USDE_SLIPPAGE_BPS <= 100`, while
   `script/interactions/governance/DeployUSDeMarketYieldStrategy.s.sol:81` sets **120 bps** for the
   **same asset over the same Curve pair** with **no preflight of its own**. Four mainnet-facing
   artifacts carry four values (**30 / 100 / 120 / 150**) and **the loosest one has no guard**.
3. **The attack is negative-sum, so damage is not gated on attacker profitability.** Below breakeven
   the protocol still loses the **full** collateral amount while the attacker also loses money — so a
   griefer, a competitor, or a holder of an offsetting phUSD short inflicts the **full** cost at **any**
   tolerance, including **120 bps and 50 bps**. Profitability gates the attacker's *willingness*, not
   the *damage*.

## Finding description and impact

**Ledger fingerprint:** `4a5a00bbcc49803e3424ba857f36bdf59f69f010d5b41e9d952669dd354eea46`

### Summary

`StableStakerV2.autoAnnihilate` pays its caller the *frictionless* value of an exit while only ever annihilating what the exit actually delivered, minting the difference as fresh phUSD. The shortfall therefore lands on the protocol, not the caller — and a **third party with no position in the contract** can manufacture that shortfall by sandwiching someone else's `autoAnnihilate` through the AMM-backed `ERC4626MarketYieldStrategy`, keeping the sandwich profit.

The only thing bounding how much shortfall can be induced is `slippageToleranceBps`, a parameter that lives in a **different repository** (`reflax-yield-vault`), is documented there as a **deposit-side** haircut control, and is never read, bounded, asserted or reported anywhere in `StableStakerV2`. That cross-repo, mis-documented coupling is the Law-3 owner footgun at the core of this finding.

### Vulnerability detail

The caller is debited the *request* and paid the *frictionless target*, at `src/StableStakerV2.sol:574-582`:

```solidity
uint256 received = _routeExit(token, gross, true);
// MEASURED DELTA. {_routeExit} returns what actually arrived, which a selling strategy
// may haircut below the request. Only what arrived is ever annihilated; the difference in
// VALUE is made up in freshly minted phUSD, never out of the idle buffer.
uint256 netUsed = received < netWanted ? received : netWanted;
annihilatable = netUsed * scale;
(phUSDPaid, phUSDMinted) = _annihilateAndTopUp(token, netUsed, netWanted, scale);
```

and the top-up itself, at `src/StableStakerV2.sol:636-648`:

```solidity
uint256 delivered = _annihilateMeasured(token, netUsed, netUsed * scale, stableMinter);

// The frictionless payout: both halves of `netWanted`, as if the exit had lost nothing. The
// single truncation lives inside `calculateMintAmount` and floors, i.e. favours the protocol.
uint256 target = netWanted * scale + stableMinter.calculateMintAmount(token, netWanted);
phUSDMinted = target > delivered ? target - delivered : 0;
if (phUSDMinted > 0) {
    require(phUSDMintAvailable(), "StableStaker: phUSD mint unavailable");
    _phUSD().mint(address(this), phUSDMinted);
}
```

`netWanted` is fixed before the interaction; `delivered` is whatever the AMM round trip produced. Widening the gap between them is worth money to anyone who can move the pool, because the strategy sells its position into that moved pool and the protocol absorbs the difference in newly minted phUSD.

The contract's own NatSpec anticipates the *self*-sandwich and accepts it, at `src/StableStakerV2.sol:496-501`:

```
 *      THE TOP-UP IS EXTRACTABLE, AND THAT IS ACCEPTED. A caller who sandwiches their own exit
 *      through an AMM-backed strategy keeps the sandwich profit AND is still paid the frictionless
 *      figure. It is bounded by the strategy's own `slippageToleranceBps` (which reverts below its
 *      floor), by a real AMM round trip, and by the caller's own stake and accrual. Shifting exit
 *      slippage off the user and onto the protocol is the explicit goal; the operational lever is
 *      `slippageToleranceBps`, set as tightly as the market allows. See CLAUDE.md.
```

Two of those three stated bounds do not apply to a third party: the attacker has **no stake and no accrual** in `StableStakerV2` — they are bounded only by the victim's position size and by `slippageToleranceBps`. The PoC asserts the attacker's position is zero (`A1 OK: sandwicher is a third party with no position in StableStakerV2`), which is what separates this from the accepted self-sandwich.

### How the loss must be sized (and why the two obvious instruments both mislead)

**The correct figure is collateral removed from the phUSD minter: ~1x the induced shortfall, with phUSD supply held constant.** On a `netWanted` of 9 999.99 USDe the PoC measures an induced shortfall of **6.5425 USDe**, and exactly that much less USDe is delivered into the minter.

Neither obvious instrument gives that number:

- **Do not headline `phUSDMinted`.** It **overstates the loss 2x**. `Antimatter.annihilate` mints phUSD for *both* halves of the annihilation, so a shortfall `d` reduces Antimatter's own mint by `2d`; the `2d` top-up restores exactly what the shortfall removed. The PoC's line item reads `13.0851` phUSD against a `6.5425` USDe shortfall — precisely 2x.
- **Do not cite the `phUSD.totalSupply()` delta.** It reads **zero** and understates the loss to nothing: `19999999999999999930000` in both the baseline and the sandwiched run.

The loss is **backing removed, not supply added**. Same phUSD out the door, less collateral behind it:

```
  USDe collateral delivered - baseline  : 9999999999999999965000
  USDe collateral delivered - sandwiched: 9993457456038511971619
  MARGINAL UNBACKED phUSD (== collateral lost, same supply): 6542543961487993381
```

### Impact

Protocol value leak through **unbacked phUSD inflation**, at ~1x the shortfall a third party can induce, repeatable in principle against any staker who uses the reward path while an AMM-backed strategy is wired.

The conditions on that impact are material and are stated here, not in a footnote:

- **V2 is undeployed.** Mainnet currently runs the frozen V1 at `0xbce8ABC0`; `StableStakerV2` is not live. This is a pre-deployment finding about code that would ship with the parameter coupling described.
- **No profitable attack is demonstrated at any *configured* value.** The empirical breakeven is `s = 142 bps` (141 bps → −0.79 USDe; 142 bps → +0.18 USDe); the highest value actually set on a strategy anywhere in the fleet is **120 bps**. ⚠ **Corrected post-review:** this bullet previously read "no profitable attack under any **documented** configuration" and was anchored **only** on an illustrative NatSpec example of 50 bps (`ERC4626MarketYieldStrategy.sol:36`). **Real deploy configs exist** — see the post-review addendum below. The conclusion survives; the anchor was wrong, and the margin is roughly **halved in nominal terms** relative to what the 50 bps anchor implied.
- **The PoC prices no capital cost.** No flash-loan fee, no inventory cost, no MEV bundle bribe. A bribe of a few tens of USDe erases the entire `s = 200 bps` profit of 51.46 USDe. The true breakeven is therefore **materially above 142 bps**.
- **142 bps is specific to these pools, this block and this victim size** — USDe/crvUSD ≈ 161k/161k, crvUSD/sUSDe ≈ 384k/300k, `netWanted = 10k`. Deeper pools raise the breakeven; larger victims lower it.
- **Repeatability and aggregate drain were not tested** — the PoC exercises a single call. Reaching the `s = 500 bps` worst case requires roughly $390k of attacker capital.
- **The attack is negative-sum.** The protocol always loses strictly more than the attacker gains (0.67 USDe of attacker profit per 1 USDe of protocol loss at the worst case). This bears on the attacker's incentive; it is not a reason to discount the protocol's loss.
- **No user loses funds.** Victims are made whole by design — the PoC shows the staker's phUSD receipt is byte-identical with and without the sandwich (`19999999999999999930000` either way). The loss is invisible to them and falls entirely on protocol backing.

### Why Medium and not High

No user asset is stolen and no user position is impaired, so this is not a High. It is a **protocol value leak with stated assumptions and an external requirement** — an AMM-backed `ERC4626MarketYieldStrategy`, and a `slippageToleranceBps` above roughly 1.4% — which is the definition of C4 Medium. The external requirement is a qualifier *for* Medium, not a disqualifier.

The reason it is not reduced below Medium is the **Law-3 footgun**: `slippageToleranceBps` lives on the strategy in `reflax-yield-vault` (and its *only* code bound is `require(_bps <= MAX_BPS)` — i.e. 100% — at `ERC4626MarketYieldStrategy.sol:88-89`), while its **actual deployed values are set by mainnet-facing scripts in `phoenix-phase-2-staging`** (see the post-review addendum). It is documented there as a control on the *deposit* haircut, and nothing in `StableStakerV2` reads it, bounds it, asserts on it or surfaces it — yet it is the sole cap on third-party-induced unbacked minting in this contract. A competent, non-malicious owner raising that value to unstick deposits would be surprised to learn they had also raised the ceiling on unbacked phUSD issuance. Surprise ⇒ footgun ⇒ in scope.

The counterweight — and the reason this is Medium rather than High — is that `slippageToleranceBps` has a **countervailing incentive**: raising it also reduces the principal credited on deposit, so an owner has an independent reason not to push it far. That is a soft economic brake, not a bound in code.

## Recommended mitigation steps

**Bound the top-up inside `StableStakerV2` itself rather than delegating the ceiling to a parameter in another repository.** The shortfall the protocol is willing to absorb should be an explicit, local, owner-set policy:

```solidity
uint256 target = netWanted * scale + stableMinter.calculateMintAmount(token, netWanted);
phUSDMinted = target > delivered ? target - delivered : 0;
require(phUSDMinted * 10_000 <= target * maxShortfallBps, "StableStaker: top-up exceeds policy");
```

This is a single local invariant that no cross-repo configuration change can silently widen, and it **also bounds L-01 / `ss18l1`** (the uncapped direct-mint path), because the same check caps how much phUSD can be issued outside `PhusdStableMinter`'s collateral and daily-cap discipline.

Secondary measures, in order of value:

1. **Read and report `slippageToleranceBps` from this side.** If the parameter is going to be the operative bound, `StableStakerV2` should read it, assert it against a locally-configured ceiling on strategy wiring, and emit it — an owner should not have to open a second repository to discover this contract's inflation limit.
2. **Fix the documentation of the parameter's dual role in `reflax-yield-vault`.** It is documented as a deposit-haircut control; it is in fact also the cap on a sibling contract's unbacked minting. Note that raising it has a countervailing effect (it reduces credited principal on deposit), which is why an owner may reasonably move it and be surprised by the second consequence.
3. **Verify the strategy's `minOut` floor anchor.** `ERC4626MarketYieldStrategy.sol:166-168` — `uint256 idealUnderlying = vault.convertToAssets(sharesToSell); uint256 minOut = idealUnderlying * (MAX_BPS - slippageToleranceBps) / MAX_BPS;` — anchors the floor to vault NAV rather than to market price, which is what opens the extractable slack of `N*(s-d-f)` in the first place.

### Margin: nominally halved, but the direction runs the *safe* way

The deployed **120 bps** sits at **~85% of the nominal 142 bps breakeven**, where the 50 bps NatSpec
example implied 35%. The comfort margin is therefore roughly **halved in nominal terms**.

**Direction matters and is easy to get backwards:** unpriced flash-loan fees, inventory cost and MEV
bundle bribes push the *true* breakeven **up** — further above 142 bps and further **away** from 120 —
so the real margin at the live setting is **wider** than the nominal 22 bps, not narrower. This is why
the evidence raises the finding to the top of Medium and no further.

### The sharpest form of the Law-3 argument: the fleet does not agree with itself

Four mainnet-facing artifacts carry **four different values (30 / 100 / 120 / 150)** for **one
parameter on one asset** — and the artifact with the **loosest** value is the one with **no guard**:
`DeployUSDeMarketYieldStrategy.s.sol` sets 120 bps, above the `<= 100 bps` ceiling its sibling
`MigrateStableStakerMainnet.s.sol:312` enforces for the *same asset over the same Curve pair*, and it
broadcasts from a Ledger. The only bound anywhere in code is `require(_bps <= MAX_BPS)` — 100%.

**Operational discipline is demonstrably not a substitute for the missing code bound.** The owner is
already operating near a line that nothing in `StableStakerV2` discloses, bounds or reports — and the
fleet does not agree on where that line is. This makes the recommended **local `maxShortfallBps`
invariant materially more urgent**: it is the one bound that no cross-repo script drift can silently
widen.

### The protocol's loss is not gated on attacker profitability

The attack is **negative-sum**, so a griefer, a competitor, or anyone holding an offsetting phUSD short
incurs the protocol's **full** loss at **any** tolerance — **including 50 bps**. `142 bps` is the
threshold for an **unsubsidised** attack, not the threshold for **harm**.

### Revisit-High trigger (human-actionable)
