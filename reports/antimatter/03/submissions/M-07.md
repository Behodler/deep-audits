## Finding description and impact

### Summary

`Antimatter.annihilate` accepts a caller-supplied slippage floor, `minPhUSDOut`, documented as the
protection a caller buys against the exchange rate moving between quote and inclusion. The floor is
denominated over the **combined** phUSD total — the antimatter half plus the stable half — but the
antimatter half is minted unconditionally on every returning path. The sum therefore can never fall
below `amount`, so the check is unsatisfiable for every `minPhUSDOut` in `[0, amount]`. A caller who
states a floor anywhere in that band is exactly as unprotected as one who passes zero, and nothing
in the source, the NatSpec, the event or the tests says so.

### Vulnerability details

`src/Antimatter.sol:258-260` — the floor:

```solidity
        // The caller's floor, measured against the total both halves come to.
        uint256 totalPhUSD = amount + mintedForStable;
        if (totalPhUSD < minPhUSDOut) revert InsufficientPhUSDOut(minPhUSDOut, totalPhUSD);
```

`src/Antimatter.sol:263` — the antimatter half, minted unconditionally three lines later:

```solidity
        _phUSD.mint(recipient, amount);
```

`mintedForStable` is an unsigned balance delta, so `totalPhUSD = amount + mintedForStable >= amount`
holds in every reachable state. Substituting into `:260`, the predicate `totalPhUSD < minPhUSDOut`
is unsatisfiable whenever `minPhUSDOut <= amount`. The guard's useful range begins one wei above
`amount`. The protection a caller actually purchases is `minPhUSDOut - amount`, not `minPhUSDOut`.

Callers land inside the inert band by reasoning correctly rather than by mistyping. The antimatter
half is fixed by the caller's own input; the **stable half is the only leg that can move**, so the
natural figure to state is the one that covers that leg — and `minPhUSDOut = amount` sits exactly on
the inert boundary. The NatSpec at `src/Antimatter.sol:222-225` reinforces the error by naming zero
as *the* opt-out, which implies every non-zero value is opt-in:

```solidity
    /// @param minPhUSDOut The least phUSD the caller will accept in total, counting both halves —
    ///        an 18-decimal phUSD quantity, never in `stable`'s decimals. Compute it off chain.
    ///        Passing zero waives the protection entirely and is permitted: at a zero exchange
    ///        rate it means giving up the stablecoin half for nothing but the antimatter half.
```

It is not. Values in `[1, amount]` waive identically to zero, and the documentation never says so.

Nothing rejects, warns about, or records an inert floor. `src/Antimatter.sol:266`:

```solidity
        emit Annihilated(stable, msg.sender, recipient, amount, stableAmount, totalPhUSD);
```

`minPhUSDOut` appears nowhere in the event, so an inert floor and an honoured one are
indistinguishable both on chain and off.

### Impact

When the settled rate is below parity, the caller's shortfall is realised and irreversible. The
stablecoin is pulled from the caller at `src/Antimatter.sol:246`:

```solidity
        IERC20(stable).safeTransferFrom(msg.sender, address(this), stableAmount);
```

and is then deposited through `PhusdStableMinter.mint` at `src/Antimatter.sol:251`, which is the step
that makes the loss irreversible — the stablecoin passes into the configured yield strategy and out
of the caller's reach:

```solidity
        minter.mint(stable, stableAmount);
```

`src/Antimatter.sol:254` then asserts the contract's own stablecoin balance returned to its pre-call
value, so no residue remains recoverable at Antimatter:

```solidity
        if (IERC20(stable).balanceOf(address(this)) != stableBefore) revert StableNotDeposited();
```

and `src/Antimatter.sol:256` measures the stable leg as an unsigned balance delta, which is why the
sum at `:259` can never fall below `amount`:

```solidity
        uint256 mintedForStable = _phUSD.balanceOf(address(this)) - phUSDBefore;
```

At the
audited pin of the nested dependency — `lib/phUSD-stable-minter` @
`d6ed11561e14d2d43adfe481e01357fbd800e218` — that minter exposes **no** redeem, burn or withdraw
entry point; its entire external surface is one-way in-flows and configuration setters. The deposit
has no exit. This statement is scoped to the pinned tree under audit and is not necessarily true of
whatever is deployed.

Worked case, verified by the PoC: `amount = 100e18` antimatter paired with `100e6` USDC, exchange
rate `R = 6e17`, caller states `minPhUSDOut = 100 ether`. `:260` evaluates `160e18 < 100e18` → false.
**The call succeeds.** The caller receives `160e18` phUSD:

- against par on the **stable half** (`100e18`), a **40% shortfall** — `60e18` delivered;
- against the **200e18 par total** of the pair, a **20% shortfall**.

At the `R = 0` endpoint the caller receives only the antimatter half and the stablecoin is consumed
for nothing.

Two qualifications, so the figure is not read as larger than it is. Where `R` correctly tracks a
genuine depeg, the nominal shortfall is a correct re-pricing and the caller's economic loss is
smaller than the 40% nominal figure; the severity here is carried on the **stale, mis-set and zero**
rate cases. And at parity (`R = 1e18`) the shortfall is exactly zero and this finding has no impact.

### How a below-parity rate arises — the claim, stated at its defensible width

`exchangeRate` is a plain stored value on `PhusdStableMinter.StablecoinConfig`
(`PhusdStableMinter.sol:27`). It has **no decay, no oracle input and no timestamp of its own** — the
only timestamp in the minter, `lastMintTimestamp`, belongs to the rolling 24h mint cap
(`PhusdStableMinter.sol:216-219`), not to the rate. It is written in exactly two places,
`registerStablecoin` (`:111-130`) and `updateExchangeRate` (`:138`), both `onlyOwner`.

The consequence must be stated precisely, because the looser version of it is wrong: **staleness does
not move the rate.** A never-refreshed rate simply sits at whatever the owner last wrote, so reaching
`R < 1e18` always requires a *prior* owner write. This finding therefore does not claim a
below-parity rate can materialise with no owner action at all.

What it does claim is the **recovery-lag** case, which needs no *further* owner action and is enough
on its own: the owner correctly lowered the rate during a depeg, the stablecoin subsequently
recovered, and the rate was never restored. Every annihilation between the recovery and the
restoring write lands below parity through pure inaction. That is an ordinary operational lag, not a
reckless act, which is what carries this finding past the reckless-admin challenge — and it does so
without leaning on the `updateExchangeRate(_, 0)` lever at all.

One further deflator, stated because it is the obvious objection: the *common* staleness direction is
stale-**high**, where a rate left above the stable's true value **over-mints**. That is a different
finding and a different victim — ledger **L-06** (`6ce80dacbcf6d566`), where the caller picks the
cheapest registered stable. Caller loss here requires stale-**low**, which is strictly the narrower
of the two directions.

### Relationship to ledger F-01 — ⚠ INCOMPLETE FIX

This finding is the residual of ledger entry **F-01** (`3aac91383dcb6060`), human-set `fix-pending`.
It is **not** a regression, and F-01 is **not** closed by story-003.

`WATCH-02-02` set F-01's closure bar in two exact fragments: closure requires evidence of
"a CALLER-SUPPLIED minimum-output parameter", illustrated as
"(e.g. `uint256 minPhUSDOut` checked as `amount + mintedForStable >= minPhUSDOut` before :260)".
story-003 implements that illustrated formulation **verbatim** —
which is precisely the problem: the bar named the defective denomination, so a defective
implementation satisfies it. The implementation is faithful to its story; the story's own intended
behaviour is the defect.

F-01's human-set `triageReason` also sanctioned deleting the protocol-side zero-mint check, on the
reasoning quoted verbatim:

> "The audit agrees the zero check carries no state risk once removed - it was a fail-fast BEFORE

> value was gas."

The Halmos result refutes that premise: over `minPhUSDOut` in `[0, amount]` **nothing reverts
post-burn**, so the call settles rather than unwinding. The deleted check was not a gas
optimisation; it was the only protocol-side liveness guard on the stable leg. Stated without blame:
the incompleteness was baked into the agreed fix plan, not introduced by the executor. It is
recorded here because it bears on reachability — the configuration that triggers the loss is the
configuration that was believed safe.

### Severity

**Medium.** There is no attacker and no permissionless path into the loss state:
`PhusdStableMinter.updateExchangeRate` is `onlyOwner`, no party receives the caller's stablecoin
(it goes to the yield strategy), and no front-run or sandwich creates the condition. At parity the
shortfall is exactly zero, so the external requirement is load-bearing. This is a missing protection
that fails against an ordinary configuration — C4's Medium limb, "leak value ... with stated
assumptions, but external requirements."

**Placement within the band.** The independent severity audit confirmed Medium with no
overstatement found, and placed this at the **top of Medium**: the victim is not a careless user but
one *actively exercising* the protection the protocol offers, and `minPhUSDOut = amount` — the
natural computation — is silently identical to passing zero.

**Escalation trigger:** if a permissionless or front-runnable route into a below-parity exchange
rate is demonstrated, this becomes High.

## Recommended mitigation steps

### 1. Reject an inert floor outright (minimal, preserves the documented opt-out)

```solidity
if (minPhUSDOut != 0 && minPhUSDOut <= amount) revert FloorCannotBind(minPhUSDOut, amount);
```

Keeps `0` as the documented waiver while turning a meaningless floor into a loud error rather than
silent non-protection. This is the smallest change that closes the gap between what the parameter
promises and what it does.

### 2. Or re-denominate the floor on the leg that can actually move

```solidity
if (mintedForStable < minPhUSDOut) revert InsufficientPhUSDOut(minPhUSDOut, mintedForStable);
```

Checking the stable leg directly removes the inert range entirely, and matches how a caller
naturally reasons about the trade. Re-document `minPhUSDOut` as a stable-leg minimum if this option
is taken.

**Caveat found by Tier 3:** a floor on `mintedForStable` alone is still satisfiable by donated
phUSD, because `mintedForStable` is an unattributed balance delta rather than an attribution of what
the minter minted for this call — phUSD arriving inside the measurement window raises the delta and
passes the floor by construction. That is ledger **L-01** (`ad4b779566291190`), which remains open
and is not closed by either option above.

### 3. Independently, restore a protocol-side zero-mint check after `:256`

```solidity
if (mintedForStable == 0) revert PhUSDNotReceived();
```

This costs no `calculateMintAmount` call: it is a post-mint **observation** of the measured delta,
not a reinstatement of the deleted pre-burn quote, so the owner's stated objection to re-deriving
the minter's quote inside `annihilate` does not apply.

This addresses ledger **L-03** (`0ed1c6e3270816c5`), a **separate root cause**. Fixing the inert
range does not fix the zero-rate case for a caller who genuinely passes zero, and fixing the
zero-rate case does not fix the inert range for every `R` in `(0, 1e18)`. Both are owed.

### 4. Record the floor on chain

Emit `minPhUSDOut` in the `Annihilated` event at `:266`, so a settled annihilation carries a record
of the protection the caller asked for. Today an inert floor leaves no trace.

### 5. Documentation

The NatSpec at `:222-225` presents `0` as *the* opt-out. It must state that **every** value at or
below `amount` waives identically, for as long as the combined-total denomination stands.

### 6. Operational guidance until a fix lands

The owner is trusted and non-malicious, and this is the footgun to avoid. The minter offers three
stand-down levers, and only one of them is lossy:

- `pause()` — reverts inside `minter.mint`, unwinding the burn whole. **Safe.**
- `setStablecoinEnabled(stable, false)` — reverts inside `minter.mint`
  (`require(config.enabled, "Stablecoin minting is paused")`), unwinding the burn whole. **Safe.**
- `updateExchangeRate(stable, 0)` — does **not** revert. The burn and the stablecoin transfer
  settle, and the caller receives only the antimatter half. **Lossy.**

Recommended sequence for re-pricing a depegged stable: **disable the stable, move the rate, then
re-enable it.** Never use `updateExchangeRate(stable, 0)` as a stop. Separately, any UI or
integrator must compute `minPhUSDOut` as `amount + expectedStableLeg` with real slippage tolerance,
and must never submit a value at or below `amount`.

## Publication note — L-03 is an appendix to this report, not a second Medium

**FLAG-04, binding on whoever compiles the run summary.** M-07 and ledger **L-03**
(`0ed1c6e3270816c5`) are **one loss channel with two orthogonal mitigations**. L-03 is currently
proposed for a low → medium re-weigh; **if a human applies that re-weigh, L-03 is published as an
appendix to this report**, carrying its own fingerprint `0ed1c6e3270816c5` verbatim — never as a
standalone Medium submission.

Any count must therefore read **"1 Medium loss channel (2 entries)"**, never "2 Mediums".

The reason is the same fact from both sides. The two share a *loss* but not a *root cause*: M-07 is
the floor's **denomination** (a combined-total floor whose larger half is unconditional), while L-03
is the deleted **protocol-side zero-mint check**. Fixing either leaves the other live — restoring the
zero-mint check is silent for every `R` in `(0, 1e18)`, and re-denominating the floor is silent for a
caller who genuinely passes zero — which is exactly why they remain separate ledger entries, and
equally why they are not separate losses. Counting them twice would double-count a single channel;
merging them would let one entry be closed on the other's patch. The appendix form is what keeps both
from happening.
